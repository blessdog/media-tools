Through the Scroll

Every technique available for moving a camera through a flat painting, what each one actually consumes as input, and which one fits the data we already built.

Resolved the same day — read this first

This survey concluded that Uni3C was the answer. Testing overturned it within hours. Wan 2.2 — the model that owns the entire control ecosystem, Uni3C included — rendered a 3D excavator breaker into the painting and repainted the horse as a cartoon. What shipped instead was smaller and better: Flux 1 Fill, run once in layer space, hard-composited into the seams the card stack leaves behind. Ryan's verdict: "flux is the way." The survey below is kept intact because its reasoning is still how the decision was reached — jump to What actually happened for the result.

The brief: one 2D image — Wang Meng, ink and colour on paper, c. 1360, no linear perspective, no photographic cues — and a camera that travels through it on x, y and z with correct parallax. Three constraints decide everything:

Content must not change. Ge Hong stays the same man. A model that redraws his face has failed, no matter how good the move looks.
Style must not change. Ink stays ink. Any drift toward photographic rendering is a total loss.
It must be reusable. This is one painting and many shots. A technique that re-rolls the dice per shot costs more every time it's used; one that produces an asset costs once.

Those constraints, not model quality, are what eliminate most of the field.

What each family is really doing

and why four of them are the wrong question

Layered depth image + inpaint

this is our baseline

Cut the picture into depth planes, paint behind each occluder once in layer space, project through a pinhole camera. Shih et al.'s context-aware LDI inpainting (CVPR 2020) and Niklaus' 3D Ken Burns are this family. It is free, infinitely re-cuttable, and temporally perfect by construction because every frame is a resampling of fixed texels.

It fails in exactly one place: a card is flat and its silhouette is a hard cut, so objects straddling a seam shear, and heads get clipped. That is the defect you named — and it is the only defect left.

3D Photography using Context-aware Layered Depth Inpainting

Continuous mesh warp

built, tested, rejected

Replace hard cards with one elastic sheet, stiff inside objects and compliant in bare silk. Solved to a median rigidity of 0.195 px with zero holes. You looked at it and said it distracted from the painting, and the measurement agrees with your eye: a similarity transform is a linear displacement field with zero Laplacian, so smoothing can never fight rigidity — the strain has to go somewhere, and where it goes is visible.

Feed-forward 3D from a single image

wrong tool, for a specific reason

TripoSplat, Apple's SHARP and Microsoft's TRELLIS turn one picture into 3D Gaussians in a single forward pass. They are object-level — the wrong scale for a landscape scroll. The scene-level ones are Flash3D, Bolt3D (under seven seconds), WonderWorld (under ten on an A6000) and FlashWorld.

Two problems. All are trained on photographs, and a painting with no perspective cues gives them nothing to reconstruct from. The published comparison notes Bolt3D produces "imprecise tree branches and needle-like leaves" — which is a fair description of the entire left half of this painting.

But the deeper problem is that this family answers a question we already answered. See below.

Camera-conditioned video diffusion

closer, still a vacuum

ViewCrafter, MultiDiff, SplatDiff, NavCrafter, CamCtrl3D, Stable Virtual Camera, and Alibaba's Wan 2.2 Fun Camera. These take a trajectory — pan up, orbit left, a pose sequence — and generate the video. Genuinely more control than plain image-to-video.

Still not enough. A trajectory tells the model where the camera goes; it does not tell the model what the scene is. Everything occluded is still invented, which is precisely how you get a different Ge Hong.

Render-and-refine / control conditioning

the fit

The model never generates the scene. It receives a rendered video as a structural control signal and repairs it, with a reference image holding identity and style. VACE, Fun-Control, and — the one that matters — Uni3C.

The thesis

we are not missing a 3D scene — we authored one

Every method in family three exists to estimate a 3D representation from a picture, because normally nobody has one. We are not in that situation. Over the last week we built, by hand and by measurement:

Eleven depth planes with authored z. Forty-nine segmented objects, each pinned wholly onto one depth so nothing straddles a seam — verified at exactly 1.00× shear on all eighteen objects that previously sheared. Every plane painted in behind its occluders in layer space, disocclusion holes down from 22.74% of the union to 0.00%. An exact pinhole camera with rest-normalised projection. A measured bare-silk map. A tear map.

That is the 3D asset. A splat model would spend a GPU-hour guessing a worse version of it.

So the correct question

Not "which model can generate a flythrough of this painting" — that is a vacuum, and models fill vacuums with invention. It is: which model accepts a hand-authored 3D render as its control signal, and repairs only what the render gets wrong?

Uni3C

alibaba damo · siggraph asia 2025 · native in comfyui since 21 july 2026

Uni3C's camera module, PCDController, is a lightweight 0.95B DiT trained against a frozen video backbone. verified It consumes exactly three things:

PCDController inputWhat it isDo we have it
Rendered point cloudsThe scene warped to each camera position and rendered to a videoYes — FLY-FINAL.mp4 is literally this
Plücker raysPer-pixel camera ray encoding, i.e. the exact camera parametersYes — render-parallax writes them into the manifest
Noisy latentInternal to the samplern/a

Its own pipeline builds that point cloud by running monocular depth estimation on frame one, then warping and rendering it. verified

That is the stage that fails us, and it is the stage we skip. Our own estimate-depth.py documents that monocular depth works on photographs and fails on ink painting — no shading, no texture gradient, no perspective for the network to read. Uni3C's weakest link is a step we replaced weeks ago with hand-authored geometry.

Two more properties matter. Uni3C reports working across domains — "real-world, text-to-image, animation" — so a non-photographic input is inside its stated envelope rather than off the end of it. verified And the ComfyUI implementation is a model patch that "works with all Wan model variants (2.1/2.2, T2V, I2V, VACE, Fun Camera, S2V, Humo, Animate)". verified

That last point is the one to hold onto. Uni3C supplies camera precision; VACE supplies masked repair plus a reference image for ink identity. They stack in one graph. We can tell the model where the camera is, exactly which pixels are broken, and what the painting looks like — three channels, all of which we generate deterministically.

Wan's licence adds one clause worth keeping: "we claim no rights over your generated contents." The output is ours outright.

There is no API and no per-render fee. The only cost is GPU hours on a box we rent, control for the duration, and destroy — $0 when it is down. No subscription, no rate limit, and no terms of service that can be rewritten under a project that will re-render this painting many times.

One assumption this reverses

Our own models/wan22-vace.json warns "rent this LAST — the most expensive thing in the catalog" at 72.2 GB. That figure is the bf16 two-expert MoE pair. The Uni3C stack totals roughly 32 GB — less than half the pull. The warning does not apply to this route.

Three caveats. Gate one is the deliberate exception — it uses a hosted API for about twenty cents, because renting a box to answer one yes/no question costs more than the answer is worth. Self-hosted means a rented Linux box, not the Mac: 24 GB VRAM minimum, and ComfyUI with Wan 14B on Apple Silicon is not a viable path. The reference workflow ships a step-distill LoRA (lightx2v_cfg_step_distill) which we drop on sight — that is the distilled tier this repo rejects, and we pay in minutes instead of quality.

No throughput measurement exists for Wan on our cards; plan-gpu knows only Hunyuan and USO. A first session on an A100 at ~$1/hr is realistically a few dollars including the download, and the honest number arrives with the first run rather than before it.

What I'd run, in this order

each step answers one question and gates the next

  1. Does Wan hold the ink at all?

    Hosted Wan 2.2 A14B via the fal route already wired into image-to-video.mjs. Roughly twenty cents. No control, no box, no downloads — this asks the single prerequisite question, and if the answer is no then nothing downstream is worth building.

    cost ≈ $0.20 · no box

  2. Does the node take our render?

    Rent a box, pull ComfyUI, query /object_info for WanUni3CControlnetApply and read its real input signature before pulling any weights. This is the ledger's one unverified row and it costs minutes.

    cost ≈ $1 · box up, no weights

  3. Emit the control channels

    A --masks flag on render-parallax writing per-frame hole and seam masks, plus Plücker rays from the camera we already solve. CPU, deterministic, no GPU. This is the work that makes the model's job repair rather than invention.

    cost: $0 · local

  4. Uni3C + VACE, one graph

    Camera from Uni3C, masked repair and ink reference from VACE, our render as the control video. Then you look at it and decide.

    cost: a box for a few hours

Steps one and two are gates, not options — each is cheap and each can kill the plan before it gets expensive. Step three is free and happens regardless. Step four is the only one that spends real money, and by then both unknowns are closed.

What does not change

The cel stack stays. Uni3C repairs the landscape seams; the figures are the thing every diffusion model on this project has proven it will redraw, and walk-figure plus composite-shot already handle them deterministically at zero churn. Model for the space, cel for the figures — that division is unchanged by any of the above, and every result here strengthens it.

What actually happened

17 aug 2026 — the gates were run, and both changed the plan

Gate 1 killed the premise Uni3C rests on

Same still, same camera-only prompt, against the Hunyuan clip already accepted on this painting. Wan 2.2 A14B produced a yellow-and-red 3D excavator hydraulic breaker across the top of frame and repainted the horse and rider as smooth vector cartoon. Attribution was checked: source clean, Hunyuan clean, Wan alone.

Two failures, and they are not the same failure. The excavator is invention in a vacuum — control conditioning genuinely fixes that class. The cartoon horse is style collapse on content that was never occluded, and no control signal fixes that, because a control signal says where things go, not what they are drawn like.

The finding worth the whole day

The model that holds this painting's ink has no control input. The model that owns the control ecosystem has a prior hostile to this material. That tension, not model quality, is what ended the Uni3C plan — every verified fact about Uni3C above remains true and none of it matters while Wan paints like that.

What replaced it is smaller and stronger

The card stack is already correct everywhere except its seams. So the model never renders a frame — it fills holes, once, in layer space, and its output is hard-composited: kept only inside our mask, everything outside copied back byte-exact.

Three things that would have cost a day each

Flux 2 is the wrong Flux. Replicate's flux-2 dev/pro/flex take reference images but no mask; there is no flux-2-fill. Flux 2 re-renders the whole frame, which is the exact mechanism that cartooned the horse. flux-fill-pro — the Flux 1 generation — takes image and mask. A mediocre prior touching 3% of pixels beats a superb prior touching 100%.

Our own style spec was aimed at the wrong picture. Ryan's distinction between 山水 (genre), 水墨 (loose ink-wash) and 工笔 (fine line) exposed it: styles/inkwash specifies "cold-press rag paper", "wet-in-wet", "blooms", "backruns", "granulation" — Western watercolour physics. This painting is dry 解索皴 unravelled-rope texture strokes with flat 設色 mineral colour and fine-line figures. That file stays untouched — it is locked and correct for its own job — and the corrected prompt now ships as the fill tool's default.

The composite bounds where, never what. Inside the mask, Flux still hallucinated a second red flower beside a real one. In layer space that invention is baked into a plane and travels with it rather than flickering — better, and not the same as harmless.
