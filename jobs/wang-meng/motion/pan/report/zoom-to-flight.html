Zoom to Flight

The Z-axis was the one unsolved axis on this painting. Two bugs were hiding under it, both now fixed and measured. What remains is a decision about whether a diffusion model enters the pipeline, and — more importantly — where.

02Two bugs, and neither was the camera

The push was a zoom — a single focal length

render-parallax took one focal length at mid-depth. Separating planes in z therefore changed their painted size — near ones ballooned, far ones shrank — so the composition came apart at frame zero. The response, recorded in the tool's own docstring, had been to throttle --z-step to 0.035. That throttle was the flatness. The stack spanned z 1.000–1.315, and a 0.22 dolly produced 1.201× of common magnification against 6.8% of differential.

No tilt value could rescue that, because tilt adds gradient within a plane on top of a between-plane budget of nearly nothing.

The fix, and its control

--plane-fit scales every world point by zr/(zr−camZ) from its own rest depth — exactly 1.0 at camZ=0 for any depth. So frame zero is the painting pixel-for-pixel however far apart the planes sit. Taking z-step from 0.035 to 0.30 (8.6× the depth range) changed zero pixels at frame zero, where without the flag it changed 56.4% of them.

The flight deformed the painting — a backing plane at maximum depth

“The problem is it's deforming the painting.”

render-parallax lays the whole source underneath as a base plane, so pixels no plane claimed still show the painting rather than a hole. That base sits one step beyond the farthest plane. Any ink the segmenter missed therefore rendered roughly twice as far away as the object it belonged to. Static, invisible. Moving, the object is magnified at two rates at once.

--plane-fit did not create this. It made a defect already sitting in the data visible — and the two effects trade directly, because every bit of depth added to kill the zoom multiplies the tearing. That is exactly why the shallow stack had looked acceptable: with 6.8% of depth there was nothing to tear with.

One guard earned its place. The tempting fix — reassign each ink component to its majority plane — flattens the stack when ink connects. On this painting a single connected component is 17.7% of all ink at a sane threshold and 94% at a loose one. probe-planes prints that number first and refuses to recommend anything; sealing by proximity makes no claim the segmentation had not already made.

03The instrument, finally

Four earlier attempts to measure this axis each failed their own control — a billboard case that is zero by construction measured 11.6, then 6.3, then 4.0, then 3.25. All four used optical flow, which on ink texture measures flow error rather than depth.

probe-zoom uses the definition instead: a pure zoom is frame 0 times one scalar. Fit that scalar by direct image matching — no correspondence step to be wrong about — and whatever residual remains is motion a zoom cannot explain. Its control, a flat still zoomed synthetically, scores 0.16/255, and the two-window differential test returns exactly 1.000 on it.

The control also caught a real bug in the harness mid-build: windows defined in frame-0 coordinates leave the frame at large scales, which pinned the first sweep near 1.0 and produced nonsense. That is the control doing its job on its author.

05The architecture you proposed has a name

“If we were to tighten fit that to an AI model… we might genuinely need to use some diffusion model. But in a very structured harnessed, regimented way.”

This is render-and-refine, also called geometry-conditioned generation: render the geometric proxy you trust, then let a model repair only what the proxy cannot produce. It is an established family, not a workaround.

Two risks are specific to this subject and both are already in the project record. Every published system above is trained on photographic scenes, so its prior pulls toward photorealism. And HunyuanVideo on this exact painting overwrote it with photographic footage in about two seconds whenever a physical phenomenon was named in the prompt. The model is not neutral about what a picture is.

06The decision that actually matters: layer space, not frame space

Every system in the table above repairs frames — 120 of them, each one a fresh chance to flicker, drift, or repaint Ge Hong into a different man. That is the expensive, fragile version of your idea.

The disocclusion holes are not a video problem. A hole appears because a plane has nothing painted behind it, and what is behind a plane does not change as the camera moves. So fill each plane once, in image space, before any frame is rendered — the Layered Depth Image approach, and what the 3D Ken Burns work does with depth-aware inpainting.

Why this is the harness you asked for

Layer-space filling puts the model exactly where the classical technique has a named limit — patch synthesis cannot invent a stroke that was never painted — and nowhere else. The trajectory stays ours, the ink stays fixed texels, and the model never sees a face.

And the hole geometry says the classical tool may cover most of it unaided: median hole radius is 6.4px and the widest is ~97px across, against clean-plate's SHIFTMAP patch synthesis which already removed a whole deer from this silk. Patch synthesis copies real silk where TELEA and Navier–Stokes smear it to mush.

07Recommended sequence

  • Extend each plane behind its occluders, classically. Compute the region of each plane hidden by nearer planes across the whole camera path, fill it with clean-plate's patch synthesis, re-render. Gate: does the hole fraction go to zero and does the fill hold up at 1:1? No GPU, minutes.
  • Fill only what patch synthesis fails on, with an image editor. The job is small and precise: given one plane with a hole where a nearer plane occluded it, paint what belongs behind — silk, more rock, the continuation of a wash. Eleven runs, no faces, no frames. See the correction below on what is actually available for this.
  • Re-segment the 14 torn components. Independent of the above and needed regardless; sealing cannot fix an object split between two real planes.

Frame-space video diffusion stays on the shelf until steps 1–3 leave something visible on the table. If they do, the target is the plane interiors — the thing a card structurally cannot have — and by then we will have a clean stack to condition it on rather than a torn one.

Correction — what the Qwen model actually is here

The first version of this report called it “the ink-wash editor we already have.” That was wrong on three counts, and the repo is unambiguous about all three.

What is genuinely true: Qwen-Image-Edit 2511 has open weights, no moderation layer, and the manifest claims native ink-wash training — which is the reason it was catalogued in the first place. That claim is unverified in the repo; nobody has rendered a frame from it.

There is also a mechanism gap I glossed. Qwen-Image-Edit is an instruction-following editor, not a masked inpainting model. Layer-space hole-filling wants a mask and a “paint only here” contract. Getting that from an instruction editor is a weaker, looser mechanism than it sounds — the honest options are a BrushNet-style masked inpainter, or compositing the editor's output back through the hole mask ourselves and accepting a seam. That is a real open question, not a detail.
