> "Don't forget it's not just about tree animation, we're still doing parallax
> lightly and tastefully." — Ryan, 2026-08-20

MOTION BEFORE CAMERA is an ORDERING, not a ban. The camera still moves; it moves
last, and it moves lightly. Read the two laws together:

- **Motion before camera** (CLAUDE.md) exists because a flight over a still
  painting is the corner being cut — parallax is cheap, fast, and *looks* like
  progress while stroke cycles are slow manual work and are the deliverable.
- **This law** exists because the correction has an opposite failure. A
  locked-off camera on a hanging scroll is not restraint, it is a different
  omission: the depth planes were built, they cost real work, and a scroll this
  tall is *made* to be travelled.

The test for a leg: **would this move still be worth watching if the living
layer were switched off?** If yes it is too much camera — it is carrying the
shot instead of accompanying it. Light means the parallax is felt and not
noticed; the leaves and the water are what the eye is on.

Practically: living cycles first, camera path after, and the LIVING GATE in
`film/compile-flight.py` already refuses a leg whose zone has no living cycles.
That gate enforces the ordering. Nothing enforces the taste, which is why this
is written down.
