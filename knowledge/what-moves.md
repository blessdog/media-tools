> "just the delicate things move. Their robes, the water ripples, leaves on
> trees." — Ryan, 2026-08-20
>
> "peaks shouldnt wobble. Think about it."

Cloth, water surface, leaves. Nothing else. Not mountains, not rock, not
buildings, not trunks, not branches, not the ground. The test is physical, not
distance-based: **would a breath of wind actually move this object?**

Two mechanisms behind it, both learned the same day:

**Stillness at distance is a depth cue, and spending it is expensive.** The near
water reads as near BECAUSE the ridge behind it is dead still. A thin uniform
wobble spread over the whole picture does not add life -- it flattens the depth
and reads as nothing moving at all, which is exactly what Ryan reported. 13
`gust-far` summit patches were built and reverted the same day.

**At summit scale a tree mass and the ridge silhouette are visually one thing**,
so animating trees up there reads as the mountain deforming.

Queued, not now: eventually the characters -- a little motion, maybe changing
facial expressions. "We'll cross that bridge when we get to it."
