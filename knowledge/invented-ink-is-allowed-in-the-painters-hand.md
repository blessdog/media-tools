## New marks are allowed. Existing marks are still untouchable.

Ryan, 2026-08-21, asked whether the servant boy could wave when both his hands
are on a shoulder pole: *"yes, to do a little wave is fine. We can invent a
little bit of ink. We invented ripples. As long as it looks hand-drawn. That
would actually be amazing. A little bird fly, you know, there's so much more we
can do as long as we keep it in Wang style."*

This retires the blanket "never invent a motion" that
[[figure-motion]] carried in its `not-when`. The constraint was never really
*displacement only* — the water ripples were drawn, not displaced, and nobody
objected, because they were drawn in the painter's hand.

**The line, and it is a different line from the one it replaces:**

| | rule |
|---|---|
| **Ink Wang Meng painted** | move it RIGIDLY. Never rotate, stretch, narrow or warp an existing stroke. |
| **Ink we add** | allowed, if it is drawn in his hand — same brush weight, same mark vocabulary, same palette. |

Both hold at once, and confusing them is the trap. [[rigid-cards-preserve-the-brushwork]]
bans DEFORMING the artist's marks; this claim permits ADDING new ones. "We may
invent ink" is not "we may now deform ink" — the original strokes are exactly as
protected as they were this morning.

**The test for an addition** is not whether it is physically plausible, it is
whether a viewer would believe Wang Meng drew it. Same brush weight as the marks
around it, the same economy, the same colour drawn from the plate rather than
picked. An addition that reads as computer graphics fails even if the motion is
correct — the same reason the per-leaf twinkle was rejected.

**Opened by this, in Ryan's words:** the boy's wave (an arm that leaves the pole,
drawn), a bird crossing (drawn, then flown as a card), *"so much more… as long as
we keep it in Wang style."*

**Unchanged:** faces still do not deform, buildings and ruled structures stay
rigid, and distant marks under ~40px stay still.
