## People look from a distance, then move toward what moves

Ryan, 2026-08-21, on the station reel: *"the way people investigate things, they
look from a distance and then slowly zoom in on certain things that are
interesting. Like if there's some movement, we can slowly move toward it. Like
the person as they wave. The way it looks now is like the same perspective. Just
floating right above, never backing out, not really zooming in."*

**The defect, diagnosed.** The reel spends fov 1.0 to 1.6 and has a push, a pull
and a parallax leg, so it is not literally a locked scale — and it still reads as
one. Two reasons, and the second is the real one:

1. 1.6x is a small range. A real approach is several times that.
2. **Every station is ONE move at ONE scale.** There is no arc *within* a
   subject: the film never establishes a thing far away and then goes to it. Nine
   independent mid-scale shots at nine locations read as one perspective sliding
   around, no matter how varied the individual moves are.

**The rule.** A subject gets a two-beat shape, not one move: **ESTABLISH wide,
then APPROACH the thing that moves.** The camera behaves like a person who has
noticed something — and what it notices is MOTION, because motion is what draws a
human eye across a still surface.

**Why this depends on animating first.** You can only move toward the movement if
the movement is already there. In a camera-first pipeline the move is chosen
before anyone knows where the life is, so the camera can only visit coordinates.
This law is the strongest argument for the animate-first inversion, and the two
should be read together.

**Which move to use is a different question**, and [[shot-vocabulary]] is the one
place that answers it — Ryan built that language for exactly this purpose and
re-sent it in full on the same day. This claim decides WHERE the camera goes and
what shape a shot takes; that one holds the named moves. Do not copy the list
into here.

**Corollary — a wide is not wasted time.** Backing out is what makes a push mean
anything, and the reel currently has almost no width to spend. The widest shot in
the film should be genuinely wide.
